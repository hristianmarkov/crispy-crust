PRODUCT VISION
Sourdough App Product Vision
A home baking companion that helps you raise a starter, plan bread around your life, read your dough, and recreate the loaves you love.
One home for every bake
Today shows what needs attention now. Feed a starter, check a rising dough, start a saved recipe or see what can wait until tomorrow. Each card shows the next action, the time window and a shortcut to log what happened.
Choose how much help you want
First Loaf mode explains each step in plain language and shows what to look for. Baker mode opens directly to formulas, timers and measurements. Switch during a bake. Start with a new starter, add an established one or join a bake already under way.
A Saturday and Sunday in the app
You want bread ready to slice at 11 on Sunday and you’re out on Saturday afternoon. The planner works backwards through cooling, baking, proofing, shaping, bulk fermentation, mixing and the starter feed. You can see exactly when you need to be home before accepting.
When the dough runs late
You log a cooler dough and less rise than expected. Shaping now looks likely to overlap your plans. The app explains the conflict, shows the options supported by your recipe and moves the remaining reminders when you pick one. If the original deadline is no longer workable, it shows the next realistic serving window.
The next loaf starts with this one
Save a crumb photo, a quick rating and what you’d change. Bake Again opens a copy of the formula and timeline with your notes, showing the measurements and choices that produced the original loaf.

01  /  STARTERS
Care for Your Starter
Know when to feed, when to check again and whether your starter is behaving as it usually does.
Create or bring your own
Start a guided culture from scratch, add an established one or accept a starter from a friend. Name each jar, save its flour mix, hydration, age and storage routine, and track offshoots when you split or share it. The beginner guide adapts to observations and explains what to look for before moving on.
Feed in a few taps
Repeat your last feed or enter the starter, flour and water weights. Calculate how much to build for a recipe, how much to retain and how much discard you’ll have. Log what you actually used, backdate missed entries and correct mistakes. Get reminders based on the jar’s own schedule.
Counter fridge and travel modes
Separate reminders for counter care, fridge storage, a pre-bake refresh and getting back into your routine after a trip. Pause or snooze one jar without affecting the others. See a low-waste maintenance plan and a discard inventory linked to recipes that use the amount you have.
Watch the rise
Log height or volume against a starting mark, add a temperature, and mark when the starter appears to peak or fall. Guided photos use the same angle and a visible scale. Compare the sequence as a chart or time-lapse. Confirm or correct suggested measurements, and label missed peaks as unknown.
See a peak window for this jar
The app combines comparable feeds for the same starter, ratio, flour and temperature. It shows when to check, how wide the estimate is and what it used to produce it. When the feed is unusual or history is sparse, the app switches to a broader estimate and asks for an observation.
Get help with an unexpected change
Describe what changed, add a photo if useful and see a short checklist for investigating timing, temperature or feeding changes. Where there may be spoilage, the app routes to reviewed guidance and never certifies a starter as safe from a photo.
02  /  PLANNING
Fit Baking Around Your Day
Start with a serving time, a start time or the step you’re already on.
Plan forwards or backwards
Plan from the moment you want to mix, put the loaf in the oven or serve it. Account for levain, rests, folds, bulk fermentation, cold proof, preheating and cooling. Show hands-on time separately from unattended time and display windows for stages that vary.
Make room for everything else
Mark sleep, work, school runs and time away. Optionally read busy blocks from a calendar without pulling in event details. Compare workable plans side by side, see which times can move, and export hands-on steps to a calendar. Save templates such as Saturday Morning or After Work.
Plan from the kitchen you have
Set the usual room and fridge temperatures, flour preferences, loaf sizes, pans, oven and equipment. The app shows which assumptions it used. Adjust a proposed plan for a colder kitchen, a different oven or the fact that you only have one Dutch oven.
Run several bakes together
Put two loaves, a focaccia and a pizza dough on one timeline. See conflicts across active doughs, oven temperature changes, vessel capacity and fridge space. Batch compatible tasks and keep each dough’s actual readings and timers separate.
Turn a recipe into a practical plan
Scale by loaf count, dough weight or baker’s percentages. Include the flour and water in the starter when calculating hydration. Convert between metric and US measures where the ingredient supports it, and flag uncertain volume conversions. Produce a shopping list, a prep list and a printable bake sheet.
Recover from a real interruption
Report a delay, missed fold, unavailable oven or slower-than-expected rise. Preserve completed steps, compare the effect of each option on the next hands-on window and the serving time, then replace upcoming reminders together. If there is no workable route in the recipe’s tested range, explain why and offer a new target.
03  /  LIVE BAKE
Follow the Dough
During a bake, the screen prioritises the next action and shows how to tell when it’s time to move on.
A kitchen friendly bake screen
Use large controls and a persistent next-step card. See the ingredient weights for the current action, tap to start or finish, and keep the screen awake during messy steps. Enable optional spoken instructions or voice logging. The core recipe, timers and journal remain available offline.
Timers that follow the bake
Keep independent timers for folds, oven phases and other active doughs. View what’s due from a lock-screen activity, widget or watch where supported. Snooze a check, complete a step early or record that you did it earlier. See missed alerts and the current state when reopening the app.
Quick observations with more detail when needed
Record dough temperature, percentage rise, texture and a photo with a few taps. Use a marked container or a sample jar for measurements, or follow recipe-specific visual cues if you don’t have a thermometer. See a side-by-side view of an earlier photo from the same stage.
Explain the next check
Distinguish the estimated timing window from the observations that suggest a step is ready. Explain why the window changed and which inputs are missing. Ask for a useful check-in instead of treating the countdown as proof that the dough is ready.
Optional hands free monitoring
Connect compatible temperature probes or add readings manually. With a phone on a stand, capture a rise sequence in a marked, straight-sided container and review the proposed rise curve. Check image quality and let the baker confirm suggested measurements before they change the timeline.
Techniques exactly when you need them
Open a short demonstration of the current fold, shaping move, scoring pattern or oven setup without leaving the bake. See left-handed views where helpful, captions and written cues. Add a private note to a step for the next time you make it.
04  /  RECIPES AND LEARNING
Explore Recipes and Build Skills
A recipe collection you can bake from
Browse everyday country loaves, sandwich bread, seeded and whole-grain formulas, rye, focaccia, pizza, bagels, enriched doughs and discard recipes. Filter by time, skill, equipment, flour, dietary ingredients and the amount of starter or discard you have.
Import and keep your own formulas
Bring in a recipe from a link, a photo of a handwritten card or pasted text. Review suggested ingredients and steps before saving. Keep the source attribution, a private editable copy and version history. Change hydration, yield or flour blend and see exactly what changed.
A course that adapts to your bread
Learn how to start a culture, read bulk fermentation, handle sticky dough, shape a loaf and use a home oven. Each lesson has a quick demonstration, a practical exercise and a shortcut back to the active bake. Follow a first-loaf course or jump to the skill you’re working on.
Find a useful substitute
See how a proposed flour or equipment substitution affects the formula and which guidance may need to change. Mark a swap as experimental when the result is uncertain. Save your favourite ingredients with flour notes and your own observations.
Keep a pantry and shopping list
Track flour, seeds and other ingredients at whatever level of detail you want. Generate a shopping list across planned bakes, check off what you already have and suggest recipes for ingredients to use up. Estimate cost per batch or per loaf from your own ingredient prices.
Make baking easier to access
Use metric or US display units, Celsius or Fahrenheit, adjustable text, high contrast, screen-reader labels and captions. Switch to a simplified step view with large targets. Print or share a formula and schedule for someone baking without a phone in the kitchen.
05  /  JOURNAL AND COACHING
Learn From Every Loaf
A journal that fills itself in
Each bake saves the formula version, planned and actual timing, observations, ingredient changes and photos you added. Record crust, crumb, flavour, texture, what you liked and whether you’d make it again. Browse by recipe, starter, date or a tag such as Best Sandwich Loaf.
Compare the bakes that matter
Put two loaves side by side and see the differences in dough temperature, hydration, flour, rise, timing and outcome. Explore charts by recipe and comparable conditions, with sample counts and missing data shown. Mark a successful loaf as the baseline for that recipe.
Ask a coach that can see the context
Ask about your current step or a specific past bake. The coach uses the recipe, measurements and notes you choose to share, points to relevant reviewed guidance and asks for missing observations when it needs them. You can approve a suggested change and see its effect before it updates the plan.
Use photos to ask better questions
Review crust, shape and crumb from a guided photo. Separate visible features from plausible explanations and offer one practical check or experiment. Compare against an earlier loaf or a curated example. When photo quality is poor or several explanations fit, say what additional information would help.
Try one change at a time
Set an experiment such as changing the flour blend or using a different shaping technique. Copy the original recipe, hold the other recorded settings constant where possible, and compare the results after baking. The app shows associations from your own bakes and leaves room for uncertainty.
Build a personal recipe book
Collect your best bakes with their formulas, notes, photos and timelines. Export a printable recipe book or a structured copy of your own bake data. Share a single bake card with photos and selected measurements, choosing exactly which details appear.
06  /  SHARING AND ADVANCED TOOLS
Bake With Others and At Scale
Share recipes that someone can actually follow
Send a recipe card that opens as a runnable bake with ingredients, instructions and timing windows. The recipient can adjust it to their schedule and save an attributed variation. Share a starter’s care card with a friend taking home a piece of your starter.
Run a shared kitchen
Invite someone to a shared bake or household space. Assign the feed, fold or oven step, acknowledge handoffs and see who logged a change. Use separate permissions for viewing and editing. Shared activity stays limited to the people you invite unless you choose to publish it.
Join a bake along
Opt into guided community bakes, small groups or skill challenges. Publish a loaf with its recipe and the measurements you choose to share so others can learn from it. Save creator recipes, follow recipe updates, and use reporting and moderation controls in public spaces.
Organise a big baking day
Use a separate production view for gifting, events and small home-bakery batches. Plan quantities and pickup windows, group doughs by oven load, print batch labels and pack lists, and estimate flour usage, batch costs and yields. Keep household and production journals separate.
Make the app yours
Pin your starter, favourite recipe or active batch to Today. Choose which reminders you receive, set quiet hours, and turn off social or coaching prompts independently. Use local-only mode or opt into encrypted transfer and sync for backups and multiple devices.
Control what leaves your kitchen
Keep journals and photos private by default. Opt in to photo analysis, calendar access, connected devices and public sharing individually. See what each shared card contains, revoke its link, export your data and delete individual records or your account.
